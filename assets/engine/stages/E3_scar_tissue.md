# E3 — Mine the world's scar tissue

### Binding · Load WHEN:
INITIATOR loads this stage immediately after E2 is signed off. Trigger: the E2 sign-off gate (E2 Step 5) is explicitly cleared and the session's Tier-1 Ledger entry contains a `## E2 Knowledge-Base — <domain name>` section with a completed failure-mode register. E3 is complete when (a) the scar-tissue protocols (or a documented NULL) are appended to the Tier-1 Ledger under `## E3 Scar-Tissue Protocols — <domain name>`, (b) `scar_protocols.md` exists at `work/<session-id>/scar_protocols.md` in one of two valid terminal states — **State A:** ≥1 externally-sourced, exhaustively-dereferenced scar protocol with verbatim-quote provenance, the `sourcing_ratio` field set, and a terminal-stamp line of the form `TERMINAL: State A (grounded) — <N> sourced / <M> speculative` present immediately after the YAML header (with accompanying `[CAVEAT: speculative scars outnumber sourced — grounding is weak; treat preventions as provisional]` line when M >= N); **State B:** a documented NULL that explicitly passed the L-A null-asymmetry pass (cited by name) with `sourcing_ratio: 0 sourced / 0 speculative` — (c) `tests/scar_tests_manifest.md` exists at `work/<session-id>/tests/scar_tests_manifest.md`, and (d) the Step 6 sign-off gate is explicitly cleared by the operator or controller. Do not load before E2 is signed off. **E10 cannot run until this stage is signed off.**

## Purpose (one paragraph)
E3 is the engine's cardinal-sin discovery pass. A fresh domain has zero lived incidents — no post-mortems from this project, no near-miss logs, no hard-won organizational scar tissue — so the structural preventions that a mature system earns through costly experience cannot simply be invented. E3's job is to substitute for that missing lived experience by mining the world's documented failures: searching external post-mortems, incident reports, known failure modes, and practitioner write-ups for this domain's analogue of the cardinal sin, then designing a structural prevention for each incident (making the error non-representable, S9-style) and a deliberately-failing RED test that must go RED when the error is reintroduced (TDD §D3). The scar protocols produced here are not predictions or anticipations — they codify what the world actually documented, grounded in external sources rather than engine priors, and explicitly framed as the best honest substitute for scars the project has not yet lived. They reduce the missing-lived-experience risk; they do not eliminate it. A fresh domain can still possess a cardinal sin that no public post-mortem has ever named, and E3 cannot protect against what the world has not written down.

## Kalshi referent (what proven mechanism this generalizes)
**D2** (`adapter/notes/10_theory_of_excellence.md` §6, D2 bullet) from the standard-workflow theory-of-excellence analysis. In the original Kalshi question-research project, the structural prevention for lookahead — the cardinal sin of backtesting — was discovered through costly lived experience: the project found that a strategy's access to settlement outcomes during backtest construction was the primary source of spurious edge, and then made lookahead structurally impossible through four interlocking mechanisms: the clock was an output (not an input) to the backtester; two walls isolated forward data from backward data; settlement was an observation (not a parameter); and a deliberately-cheating test injected the lookahead bug and asserted the harness went RED. This pattern — *find the domain's cardinal sin, make it non-representable, prove prevention with a RED test* — is domain-general, but in the Kalshi project the four concrete mechanisms were discovered by living through the failure. D2 names the compensating mechanism for a fresh domain: a dedicated external discovery pass finds the analogue of lookahead from world post-mortems, then designs the S9-style prevention and the TDD §D3 RED test. Generated protocols cite "codifies documented failure mode X from source Y" as provenance instead of a lived incident — the only honest way to fill the scar-tissue slot without scars. The pattern — *substitute documented world incidents for the scars you have not yet lived* — applies unchanged to any domain. Companion referents: **adapter/notes/10_theory_of_excellence.md §5.5** (the single largest lived-experience gap); **adapter/notes/07_adapter_design.md §4** (S9 = the error made structurally impossible + mutation-tested; the pipeline step implementing S9 is I3, which appears in §3); **protocols/TDD_AND_CODE_INTEGRITY.md §D3** (inject the deliberate bug, assert RED).

## Protocol (the steps the INITIATOR executes)

### Step 1 — Seed from E2's failure-mode register

Re-read the session's Tier-1 Ledger entry from the file — not from context. Locate the `## E2 Knowledge-Base — <domain name>` section and extract the full failure-mode register (Target D). Place the domain name and the failure-mode register at the top of working context. Record the domain name as the first line of the Step 5 Ledger entry before beginning Step 2.

From the failure-mode register, classify each entry:

- **Sourced entries** — failure modes carrying an external citation from E2's research. These are the primary seed: E3 deepens each into a structural prevention + RED test in Steps 3 and 4.
- **`[engine-inferred]` entries** — failure modes extrapolated from engine priors in E2. These are incident hypotheses. Step 2's external discovery pass must attempt to locate an external source for each. If an external source is found, the entry is upgraded to sourced. If not, the incident is tagged `[speculative-scar — no documented incident]` in E3 — distinguishing an engine-hypothesized failure from a codified real one. NOTE: the tag `[engine-inferred]` in E3 is reserved exclusively for the PREVENTION DESIGN of a real, externally-sourced incident (Step 3 case 2: incident sourced, prevention engine-designed) — it is never placed on an incident provenance line when no external source exists.
- **Thin or absent register** — if E2's register contains fewer than two sourced entries, Step 2's discovery pass bears the full weight of establishing the domain's known failure modes from scratch. Note the thin seed explicitly at the top of the Step 5 Ledger entry.

### Step 2 — External cardinal-sin discovery pass

Execute a dedicated external discovery pass to find this domain's documented cardinal sins. This pass searches the world's written record — post-mortems, incident reports, known failure mode catalogs, practitioner analyses, academic studies, regulatory findings, forensic reviews — for failures that are:

- **Structural** (not one-off human error): a recurring mechanism, a systemic design flaw, or a class of error that reappears independently across organizations
- **Domain-specific**: arising from the domain's particular microstructure, not from generic project management failures that would apply to any project
- **Non-obvious**: the kind of failure that produces plausible-looking results while the error is active — making it dangerous precisely because it is hard to notice

**The driving question for this pass:** What is this domain's analogue of the cardinal sin — the class of error that looks correct on the surface, produces results that feel like evidence, and is only recognized as structural contamination after the damage is done?

**Sourcing requirement:** Use external sources — documented incidents with named, locatable sources — as the primary input to this pass. Engine priors may generate hypotheses about where to search, but the INCIDENT (the documented failure itself) must trace to a real external source. A structural prevention may be engine-designed (tagged `[engine-inferred]` on the prevention field) when the post-mortem documents the failure but not the countermeasure — but the incident provenance must still carry an external source and a verbatim quote. An incident for which no external source is located must be tagged `[speculative-scar — no documented incident]`, kept separate from sourced protocols, and never presented as codifying a real documented failure. The binary acceptance check (check 3) enforces this: a protocol missing a named external source on its incident must carry `[speculative-scar — no documented incident]`, not a sourced provenance line.

For each incident located:
1. **Name the incident** — a short, unique label that identifies the failure mode and its source reference (e.g., "Type-confusion failure at inference time (Source: [post-mortem title])").
2. **Describe the mechanism** — how the failure unfolds: the trigger, how it propagates, why it produces plausible-looking results, what damage it causes when undetected.
3. **Cite the source** — a specific, named, locatable external source (a URL, paper title and venue, post-mortem document name, regulatory report citation). A generic attribution ("industry practice") does not satisfy this requirement; a specific source does.

Where a suspected cardinal sin is not documented in any located external source, note the gap explicitly and file the entry as `[speculative-scar — no documented incident]`. Do not fabricate a citation. A known gap explicitly documented is a catch-point for the operator's domain expertise (E1); a fabricated citation is a hallucination under L-E.

**Source-dereference requirement (E3 run-time obligation):** For every cited external source, include a RETRIEVABLE LOCATOR — a URL, DOI, archive link, or complete bibliographic citation sufficient to independently re-fetch the source — and capture the relevant excerpt as a preserved snapshot in the Ledger entry at E3 run time, so the evidence survives source movement. Additionally, EVERY cited source must be ACTUALLY DEREFERENCED at E3 run time — exhaustively, not by sampling: the INITIATOR follows the locator for each sourced protocol, confirms the source is reachable, and confirms the cited excerpt is present in the source. Confirming a citation is real is a deterministic check with no legitimate reason to sample: a fabricated but unsampled source passes undetected unless every locator is followed. This dereferencing is not a post-hoc annotation — it happens during E3 execution. This frozen protocol mandates it; the INITIATOR bears the run-time obligation. Record in the Ledger entry (under `## E3 Scar-Tissue Protocols — <domain name>`) that every cited source was dereferenced and confirm each was reachable and contained the cited finding. A sourced scar whose locator cannot be dereferenced (dead link, or source content does not contain the quoted finding) must be downgraded to `[speculative-scar — no documented incident]` or dropped — it may not remain in the sourced protocols section. A citation that is specific-looking but not retrievable via its locator, or whose content does not match the quoted finding upon dereferencing, fails acceptance check 8 regardless of how complete the provenance form appears.

**Stop criterion (prevents infinite regress):** The discovery pass is complete when two successive searches on different query strategies yield no new *structural* failure category. A structural category is a distinct mechanism — not a variation or sub-case of an already-recorded mechanism. Once this saturation signal is reached, proceed to Step 3. Do not continue searching after saturation; this is the L-D freeze posture applied to research depth.

**When the pass returns "no cardinal sin found":** A null result from the discovery pass is the most flattering possible result — it implies the domain is free of structural failure modes. Under L-A (adversarial posture), a flattering null is a load-bearing claim that requires extra scrutiny. Before accepting a null result, apply at least one additional query strategy targeting a different framing of the domain's failure space (e.g., search for near-misses rather than post-mortems, or for regulatory findings rather than practitioner write-ups). Record this asymmetry pass in the saturation log. If the null survives, proceed — but note in the Step 5 Ledger entry that the null was subjected to an L-A asymmetry pass.

**Saturation log (mandatory — the auditable stop-criterion evidence):** For each search run during this pass, record:
1. The query strategy used (what was searched for, in what source type)
2. Whether it yielded a new structural failure category (YES / NO)
3. The source(s) located

Append this log to the Tier-1 Ledger entry under `## E3 Scar-Tissue Protocols — <domain name>` as a `### Discovery saturation log` subsection, placed immediately before the protocol entries. A reviewer must be able to identify the two consecutive no-new-category searches from the log. Without this log, acceptance check 1 fails.

### Step 3 — Design a structural prevention for each incident (S9-style)

For each incident identified in Step 2 (and each E2-seeded sourced incident), design a structural prevention using the **S9 principle: make the error non-representable, not merely flagged.** A check that catches the error after it is committed is weaker than a structure that cannot represent the error in the first place. Where both are achievable, prefer the structural form (the non-representable design) and document any downstream check as a fallback.

The S9 question for each incident: *"What structural choice — in data model, interface contract, naming convention, protocol step ordering, or state machine — would make this error literally unrepresentable in a correctly-built system? What is the domain's analogue of the cardinal sin made architecturally impossible — the structural form that forecloses the error rather than catching it after the fact?"*

<example>
In a time-series backtesting domain, the cardinal sin was lookahead — access to future settlement data during history construction. The structural answer involved three interlocking mechanisms: the clock as an output (not an input) to the backtester; two explicit walls isolating forward data from backward data; settlement as an observation (not a parameter). Each mechanism made lookahead non-representable in a correctly-built system. Apply the same structural question to the domain at hand — the domain-specific analogue will differ, but the structural form (non-representable by construction, not merely detectable by check) is universal.
</example>

Two cases for the `[engine-inferred]` tag on the prevention:

- **Incident sourced, prevention from source:** If the external post-mortem or incident report documents a specific countermeasure, adopt it and note it in the protocol. The prevention is sourced.
- **Incident sourced, prevention engine-designed:** If the incident is externally documented but the post-mortem does not specify a structural countermeasure, the structural prevention is designed by the engine from the incident's description. Tag the prevention field `[engine-inferred]` — the incident is real and external, but the prevention design is the engine's own inference and should be reviewed by the operator as a proposal, not accepted as a proven fix.

An `[engine-inferred]` prevention tag does not disqualify the protocol. It signals that the structural prevention is a hypothesis to be validated, not a codified solution — and the RED test in Step 4 is especially important for `[engine-inferred]` preventions, because it is the only way to verify the prevention is catching what it claims to catch.

### Step 4 — Write a deliberately-failing RED test specification for each incident (TDD §D3)

For each scar protocol (incident + structural prevention pair), write a RED test specification. A RED test for a scar protocol is the mutation-check proof that the structural prevention is real and not decorative: it deliberately introduces the cardinal sin (the controlled cheat), and asserts that the structural prevention makes the system go RED when the sin is present.

A complete RED test specification for a scar protocol has four sub-fields:

1. **Test name** — a clear identifier tied to the incident (e.g., `test_<incident_label>_structural_prevention_fires`). The name should be unambiguous about what failure mode is being probed.
2. **Injects** — the deliberate introduction of the cardinal sin: what incorrect state, wrong ordering, forbidden data access, or type-confused input the test sets up to create the error condition. This is the controlled cheat: the test builds exactly what the structural prevention is supposed to prevent.
3. **Expected failure** — what the structural prevention asserts and why the test goes RED when the prevention is present and working. Describe the assertion that the prevention raises, catches, or blocks — the specific mechanism by which the structural prevention converts the injected sin into a test failure.
4. **Anti-cheat guarantee** — what a GREEN result on this test means. A GREEN result means the structural prevention is absent or broken — not that the system is safe. This field exists to prevent the test from being read as a passing health-check; it is a fault-injection test, and GREEN is the alarm.

The RED test is not optional. Without a RED test, a structural prevention is an assertion without evidence (L-E). An `[engine-inferred]` prevention without a RED test is a double-unsupported claim and is especially fragile.

Where the structural prevention is `[engine-inferred]`, the RED test specification is likewise speculative — label it `[engine-inferred]` alongside the prevention. It is still required.

### Step 5 — Compose and persist the scar-tissue protocol entries

For each incident, compose a scar-tissue protocol entry using the six-field format below. Every field is required. A protocol entry with any missing field fails acceptance check 2.

```
## Scar Protocol: <Incident Name>

**Provenance:** codifies documented failure mode "<mechanism summary>" from source <specific external citation> — RETRIEVABLE LOCATOR: <URL, DOI, archive link, or complete bibliographic citation sufficient to independently re-fetch the source> — QUOTE: "<verbatim excerpt preserved from the source at E3 run time — e.g., 'Section 3.2: the system failed because X' or '[source title] p.7: [finding]'>"; the QUOTE field is a source snapshot captured at run time and preserved here so the evidence survives source movement; this quote is the auditor's verification that the source actually documents the named failure mode, not merely that the source exists
[OR, for incidents with no external documentation: [speculative-scar — no documented incident] — the engine hypothesized this failure from priors; no external source located; keep this entry in a separate SPECULATIVE PROTOCOLS section after all sourced protocols; never present as codifying a real documented failure]

**Incident mechanism:** <how this failure unfolds; the trigger, propagation, and why it produces plausible-looking results while present>

**Structural prevention (S9-style):** <what makes this error non-representable; the structural choice that forecloses the error — the domain's analogue of the cardinal sin made architecturally impossible>
[Add [engine-inferred] to this field if the prevention design is the engine's inference and not documented in the source]

**RED test specification (TDD §D3):**
- Test name: <test_<incident_label>_structural_prevention_fires>
- Injects: <what the test deliberately introduces — the controlled cheat that creates the error condition>
- Expected failure: <what the structural prevention catches; why the test goes RED when the prevention is working>
- Anti-cheat guarantee: <a GREEN result on this test means the structural prevention is absent or broken — not that the system is safe>

**Consumed by:** E10 — rendered into the emitted scaffold as a structural constraint + test stub
```

**Count calibration:** There is no floor on the number of protocol entries — produce as many as the external discovery pass locates. There is no ceiling — do not truncate the domain's known failure modes to fit an arbitrary limit. The stop criterion (two consecutive no-new-category searches) governs when to stop searching, not how many protocols to produce. Two valid terminal states exist: **State A** — one or more externally-sourced, exhaustively-dereferenced scar protocols; **State B** — zero externally-sourced incidents, recorded as a documented NULL (see Step 2 null-result handling). Do not substitute `[speculative-scar — no documented incident]` entries with false provenance lines to reach State A; a speculative-only register is neither State A nor State B. **Speculative-tagged scars (`[speculative-scar — no documented incident]`) do NOT count toward State A's "≥1 sourced protocol" threshold** — only externally-sourced, exhaustively-dereferenced protocols do. A register in which every "sourced" entry subsequently fails dereferencing and is downgraded to speculative is no longer State A; it reverts to a speculative-only register (neither State A nor State B), and the null path (State B with L-A asymmetry pass) must be followed.

**Terminal state declaration (State A transparency requirement):** When `scar_protocols.md` resolves to **State A**, the file must open immediately after the YAML header (before the first protocol entry) with a terminal-state stamp in exactly this form:

`TERMINAL: State A (grounded) — <N> sourced / <M> speculative`

where `<N>` is the count of externally-sourced, exhaustively-dereferenced protocols and `<M>` is the count of `[speculative-scar — no documented incident]` entries. **"State A" must never appear in this file without the ratio beside it** — the ratio is the operator's un-maskable grounding signal. When `M >= N` (speculative entries equal or outnumber sourced entries), the stamp must be followed immediately by a prominent caveat line:

`[CAVEAT: speculative scars outnumber sourced — grounding is weak; treat preventions as provisional]`

This caveat is not optional when the condition is met; it must not be buried in a footnote or appended at the end of the file.

**Sourced-vs-speculative ratio (required terminal output):** After composing all protocol entries, record the following line in the Step 5 Ledger entry and in the YAML header of `scar_protocols.md`:

`sourcing_ratio: <N> sourced (externally documented incidents) / <M> speculative (no documented incident) out of <N+M> total protocols`

This ratio is the operator's grounding signal: a register with zero externally-sourced scars and M speculative entries is a hypothesis set, not a scar-tissue codification, and must be treated accordingly. The ratio report is not optional; acceptance check 6 enforces it.

**Persist** the full set of protocol entries in three locations:

1. Append all entries to the Tier-1 Ledger entry under `## E3 Scar-Tissue Protocols — <domain name>`, after the `### Discovery saturation log` subsection. This is mandatory under L-B — volatile context is insufficient; the protocols must survive a context reset.
2. Write `work/<session-id>/scar_protocols.md` — the standalone file for E10's consumption. Use the same six-field format; add a YAML header identifying the file as E10's input. Where E2's failure-mode register had thin coverage explicitly noted, carry that notation forward as a comment before the first protocol entry.
3. Write `work/<session-id>/tests/scar_tests_manifest.md` — one entry per RED test, stating (a) the test name, (b) what it injects, and (c) its anti-cheat guarantee in plain English. This is the operator audit layer: the operator can verify the tests assert things that actually matter, without reading code.

The YAML header for `scar_protocols.md`:

```yaml
---
domain: <domain name>
session: <session-id>
produced_by: E3
consumed_by: E10 (structural constraints + test stubs rendered into emitted scaffold)
sourcing_ratio: <N> sourced (externally documented incidents) / <M> speculative (no documented incident) out of <N+M> total protocols
terminal_stamp: "TERMINAL: State A (grounded) — <N> sourced / <M> speculative" [replace N and M with actual counts; append "[CAVEAT: speculative scars outnumber sourced — grounding is weak; treat preventions as provisional]" when M >= N; use "TERMINAL: State B (null) — no externally-sourced incidents" for State B]
sourcing_note: sourced entries carry verbatim-quote provenance with retrievable locator and preserved run-time snapshot; speculative entries tagged [speculative-scar — no documented incident] in a separate SPECULATIVE PROTOCOLS section; [engine-inferred] tag reserved for prevention design only — never on incident provenance line; every cited source was dereferenced exhaustively at E3 run time — no source exempted from dereferencing (see Ledger entry for per-source dereferencing record); any source that failed dereferencing (unreachable locator or content mismatch) was downgraded to [speculative-scar — no documented incident] or dropped
---
```

### Step 6 — E3 sign-off gate (hard gate before E10)

E3 is signed off when all of the following conditions are met and explicitly confirmed by the operator or controller:

**(a)** The Tier-1 Ledger entry contains `## E3 Scar-Tissue Protocols — <domain name>` with a `### Discovery saturation log` subsection (including at least two consecutive no-new-category search entries) and the full set of protocol entries.

**(b)** `scar_protocols.md` exists at `work/<session-id>/scar_protocols.md`, contains the YAML header (including the `sourcing_ratio` field), and records one of two valid terminal states:
  - **State A (sourced):** at least one complete protocol entry (all six fields present) backed by a real external incident with verbatim-quote provenance.
  - **State B (documented NULL):** no externally-sourced incidents found; an explicit NULL record stating "no cardinal sin documented for this domain in the discovery pass" is present; the L-A asymmetry pass was run and recorded (citing law L-A by name) before the null was accepted; and the file carries `sourcing_ratio: 0 sourced / 0 speculative` in its YAML header.
  A file containing only `[speculative-scar — no documented incident]` entries is neither State A nor State B and does not satisfy this condition.

**(c)** `tests/scar_tests_manifest.md` exists at `work/<session-id>/tests/scar_tests_manifest.md` with one entry per RED test, each naming the test and stating its anti-cheat guarantee in plain English.

**(d)** No protocol entry carries a false provenance line. Every externally-sourced entry names its specific external source AND includes a verbatim quote establishing the source documents the named failure mode. Every externally-sourced entry's provenance line includes a RETRIEVABLE LOCATOR (URL, DOI, archive link, or complete bibliographic citation sufficient to independently re-fetch the source) and a preserved source snapshot captured at E3 run time; every cited source has been dereferenced exhaustively at E3 run time and the Ledger records the dereferencing outcome for each; any source that could not be confirmed reachable or whose content did not match the quoted finding was downgraded to `[speculative-scar — no documented incident]` or dropped before finalizing the protocol set (acceptance check 8). Every incident without an external source is tagged `[speculative-scar — no documented incident]` and kept in a separate SPECULATIVE PROTOCOLS section. Every prevention designed by the engine without documentary support in the source is tagged `[engine-inferred]` on the prevention field only — not on the provenance line. No entry presents an engine-hypothesized failure as a documented external incident.

**(e)** The operator or controller reviews the scar protocols and the tests manifest and gives explicit sign-off.

**Hard gate: E10 cannot run until E3 is signed off.** E10 renders the structural preventions and RED test specifications into the emitted scaffold as structural constraints and test stubs. Without the scar-tissue protocols, E10 emits a scaffold with no structural constraints against the domain's known failure modes — the primary function of E3 in the engine. A scaffold built without structural constraints is the domain-agnostic equivalent of a system built without any structural prevention for its cardinal sin: it looks complete and produces results, but the results are structurally contaminated by whatever failure mode was never foreclosed. This gate is not a formality.

## Honest ceiling
E3 does not guarantee that the scar-tissue protocols cover the domain's actual cardinal sin. The world's documented failure record has a systematic bias: it over-documents failures that are publicly embarrassing, legally disclosable, or common enough to recur across many organizations. It under-documents failures that are novel (no post-mortem exists because no organization has yet survived the failure to write one), proprietary (the incident happened inside a company that suppressed disclosure), or structurally normalized (the failure mode is so pervasive in the domain that it is not recognized as a failure at all — it is simply "how things are done"). The most dangerous cardinal sin in a domain — the one most likely to destroy a project — is often the least documented one. E3 can only codify what the world wrote down. A novel failure mode, a proprietary incident, or a sin so pervasive it has no entry in any post-mortem will not appear in the discovery pass, will not receive a structural prevention, and will not have a RED test. The engine has no lived run to discover it. This is the honest ceiling: **world-incident mining is the best honest substitute for scars, not a substitute for the scars themselves.** A green E3 sign-off is a complete *process* over the evidence the world's public record provided — not a proven-correct model of the domain's full failure space, and not a guarantee that the domain's most dangerous sin has been named. The operator's domain expertise, surfaced in E1 and deepened in E2, remains the primary check for sins the public record has not named: if the operator knows of a domain failure mode not captured in E3's protocols, that knowledge must be recorded explicitly in the Ledger, not deferred to a future run.
